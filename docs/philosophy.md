# Keep the plan on the desk

I wanted a device that would keep me close to the tasks and projects I had chosen to work on. Checking a timer or the next meeting should be a small action. On my laptop or phone, it too easily becomes a doorway into something else.

## A physical place for a small decision

The puck gives that moment its own surface. A wheel chooses a task or a duration. A tap begins a focus block. The screen stays on the desk, holding the work in view while the laptop does the work. It is an attempt to make returning to the plan easier, not a claim that another gadget will create discipline.

## Three pieces with clear jobs

TrevOS supplies the physical language: the launcher, wheel, settings, feedback and shared services. Pomodoist holds a task and a focus interval. Cal shows the shape of the day and the next calendar event. They share a device without taking control away from each other. A calendar meeting does not prevent me from starting a timer.

The plan remains mine. Todoist and Google Calendar supply the chosen tasks and events; this firmware does not infer priorities, grade my focus or decide what I should do next.

## Open enough to take apart

Someone may want the complete device. Someone else may want a timer core, a calendar face, or the way the wheel and screen move together. The repository gives those pieces names, homes and dependency notes. Shared code stays shared, and the source, design rules and examples are available to adapt.

The public repository is where these products continue to develop. The first release is an MVP, with real constraints and further hardware testing to do. Making those limits visible is part of making the project useful to the next person.
